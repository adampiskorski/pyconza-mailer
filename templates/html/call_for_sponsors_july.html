What sponsorship pays for
Hi everyone,
PyConZA is a nonprofit conference run by the community, organised by volunteers and the Python Software Society of South Africa. We don't have a corporate budget behind us, so sponsorship is what makes the event happen. This year we need your help finding it.
Your support funds three things.
  • Beginner's Day, a 1-day workshop that runs the whole day before the main conference, introducing newcomers to Python. No experience needed.
  • Bus Factor, our initiative to build a deeper, more resilient Southern African Python community.
  • General costs of running the conference itself, including venue hire and the hybrid streaming that lets people join online from anywhere. Ticket sales only cover some of the overall costs of running the event.
What is Bus Factor, and why does it matter?
"Bus factor" is a term from project management that means how many people would need to disappear before a project falls apart? A low bus factor for the Python community means a community depends on too few people, and that's risky.
Our Bus Factor initiative raises that number. It supports more people across Southern Africa to get involved in Python, contribute, and grow into leaders, so our community doesn't rely on just a handful of people. The page is live now, and people can already apply.

How we add value to our sponsors
PyConZA is a well established conference and we've been going for around 15 years now, it's the cornerstone of the SA Python community. Generating goodwill in this community pays dividends. We shine a light on our sponsors and their contributions and we have special benefits for those who are trying to hire. Sponsoring PyConZA is also a good way to feed the ecosystem — for companies that work with Python, a thriving Python ecosystem and community will benefit everyone in the long term, sponsorship helps water the garden we all rely on.
